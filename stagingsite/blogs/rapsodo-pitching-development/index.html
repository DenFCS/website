Play with Precision: How We Use Rapsodo at The Den to Build a Pitcher's Arsenal

Inside the pitch design program that trades guesswork for objective, repeatable data on every rep.

July 23, 2026 · Tyler Beede, Den x FCS CEO, Former MLB Player · 3 min read

Pitching development used to run mostly on the coach's eye and his feel. A coach watched a bullpen and made a judgment call — "that slider popped out" — and the pitcher adjusted based on a cue given or instinct. It worked, but it left a lot to guesswork about what may be good or bad about the characteristics of the pitch.

At The Den x FCS, our pitch design and development program runs on a different standard: trusting a coach's eye paired with the precision of new-school data. With Rapsodo, every pitch thrown in our facility generates real, objective data — and that data is what drives how we build an arsenal, refine mechanics, and track progress.

What Precision Looks Like

Rapsodo gives our coaches a full profile on every pitch: velocity, spin rate, spin efficiency, spin axis and tilt, horizontal and vertical break, release height, and extension. Instead of guessing whether a breaking ball "looks" good, we can see exactly how it's spinning and how that shape compares to what we're trying to build, or maintain.

Two pitches can look similar to the eye and play completely differently against a hitter. Precision means we design around what the data shows — not just what we see.

Building the Arsenal

Every pitch serves a purpose, and Rapsodo lets us design toward that purpose instead of discovering it by accident.

  • Fastball — Spin efficiency and approach angle tell us how much perceived ride a fastball will show hitters.
  • Breaking balls — Spin axis and tilt reveal whether a slider or curveball holds its shape or flattens out.
  • Changeups — Velocity separation and spin mirroring against the fastball determine how well it disguises itself.

Rather than hoping a pitch "gets there" eventually, we identify exactly what needs to change — grip, wrist angle, release point — and confirm the adjustment worked on the next rep.

Case Study: Ryan Weiss

Ryan Weiss, a right-handed pitcher now in the Houston Astros organization, is a strong example of precision-based development in action.

Working with our staff and our Rapsodo, Ryan focused on improving spin efficiency across his pitch mix — tightening the shape and consistency of his breaking pitches rather than just chasing raw spin numbers. That work translated directly into better command: with a clearer picture of what each pitch was actually doing, Ryan was able to execute more consistently around the zone instead of relying on feel alone.

The improvement wasn't just one number going up — it was connecting the data to execution, making targeted adjustments, and confirming they held up under real reps.

Why It Matters for Every Player

This approach isn't limited to pro-level pitchers. For any player in our program, precision-based development means fewer wasted reps, faster and clearer progress, and reduced risk from chasing the wrong adjustment based on feel alone.

Precision Is the Standard

Rapsodo isn't a gadget we bring out occasionally — it's built into how we coach every session at The Den x FCS. It's how we turn "that looked better" into something we can confirm, repeat, and build on.

The Den x FCS is proud to partner with Rapsodo to bring data-driven pitching development to every player in our program.
